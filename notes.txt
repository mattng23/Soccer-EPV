In this project, I am wanting to create a metric similar to VAEP. 

However, there are limitations on VAEP that I don't like.
-Takes into account only previous 3 actions
-VAEP uses the probabilty of scoring in the next 10 actions. I however am going to use probabilty of scoring over the remainder of the possession. 

We’re trying to build a metric that estimates how much attacking value each soccer player creates with their actions.

The key idea is that a player should get credit for making the team’s attacking situation more dangerous, 
even if the possession does not end in a goal or even a shot. For example, if Messi beats several defenders and squares the ball to a striker in front of goal, Messi should still receive a large positive value even if the striker completely wastes the chance.

To do this, we want to estimate the value of a game state:

V(s)=expected attacking value of the current situation

Then we compare the state before and after a player’s action:

Action Value= V(safter​)−V(sbefore​)

So actions like progressive passes, dribbles, carries,
and dangerous receptions can receive positive value if they move the team into a more threatening situation.

The StatsBomb event data gives us the sequence of actions, who performed them, 
where they started and ended, and what eventually happened. We’ll use many possessions to learn what kinds of situations 
typically lead to high-quality scoring opportunities. 
Once the model can estimate V(s), we can assign value to individual actions and then aggregate those action values to evaluate how much attacking value each player contributes overall.
